# Comedy video production guide

How to make our comedy video about the dispatch system we built for Bahn's Spread challenge at Junction 2026: the gag
library, how to film and edit fast, and what to check before you upload. The script it refers to (the acts, and the
CAM, VO, TEXT, B-roll and AI-insert lines) is not part of this file.

## Gag library

Drop one of these into any spot where the energy drops.

- **Lying subtitles.** You say "fair wages", the caption reads "[legally sufficient wages]". Works anywhere you
  mention drivers.
- **The label-and-explode.** Any boring object gets a label ("LATE DRIVER", "GAME THEORY", "OUR SLEEP") and is hit
  by a second label ("OUR ALGORITHM", "THE COMPETITOR", "HOUR 31") with a stock explosion overlay.
- **Freeze frame + record scratch + "Yep, that's us."** Use once, on a failure in Act 3.
- **"Narrator: it did not."** You say "This will definitely work", cut to black, a deep VO says "It did not." Put it
  before strategy 2.
- **The driver running gag.** Your teammate at the bus stop. Every time he appears, he has one more layer of clothing
  on and the same sign.
- **Snap zoom** on a face with a bass boom, right after a deadpan line.
- **Corporate stock photo energy.** You and a teammate doing a hard handshake in front of the whiteboard. Hold it too
  long.
- **A fake legal disclaimer** scrolling fast at the bottom during the results: "Past margins do not guarantee future
  margins. All euros are game euros."
- **The hacker in-joke.** A "429 Too Many Requests" error pops up over your face mid-sentence; you wave it away.
- **The fee ticker.** A tiny counter in the corner going up by €0.10 every time a driver is on screen.

Sound does half the comedy: put an effect on almost every cut (whoosh, boom, ding, splat, record scratch). Use the
effects and music library in your editor, or YouTube's free Audio Library. No copyrighted songs: they get videos
muted or blocked.

## How to film and edit fast

Film with the phone, record sound close to your mouth, and edit in CapCut desktop (free, with auto-captions, text
styles and a built-in effects library). If someone on the team already knows DaVinci Resolve or Premiere, use that
instead: knowing the tool beats a better tool.

### Filming the talking head (CAM)

- Use the phone's back camera, not the USB cam: it's much sharper. Shoot 1080p at 30 fps, landscape, at eye level on
  a stack of books.
- Sit facing a window, or put a lamp behind the laptop pointing at your face. Never have the window behind you.
- Background: one tidy wall, a plant and something "corporate" (a mug, a framed printout of a chart). Tidy beats
  busy.
- Record every CAM line in script order, 3 takes each, without stopping the recording. Clap once at the start so you
  can find it.
- Deadpan wins. Say lines slightly slower than normal and hold still for one second after each line; that pause is
  where the edit puts the joke.

### Sound

- If the mic is a USB mic, plug it into the laptop and record audio there; clap to sync it to the phone video.
  No mic: put a second phone just out of frame and record a voice memo.
- Record all VO lines in one go, close to the mic, in a small room with soft things (a wardrobe full of clothes is a
  free studio).
- Kill fans and fridges while recording. Room noise is the one thing you can't fix later.

### B-roll

- Screen recordings: use the OS screen recorder (Win+G on Windows, Cmd+Shift+5 on Mac). Zoom the browser to 150%
  first so numbers read on a phone screen.
- Spaghetti: cook it soft, film in slow motion (120 or 240 fps) against a plain wall, from the side. Put a bin bag on
  the floor. Do three throws. One strand sticks if it's well cooked and the wall is smooth.
- Teammate-driver shots: 4 quick clips outside, 10 seconds each, one more layer of clothing each time.

### Editing order (don't skip ahead)

1. Lay the CAM takes, keep the best of each, and cut every pause shorter than feels natural.
2. Drop in VO lines and B-roll in script order. Fill gaps with black and white TEXT.
3. Add the AI inserts as they arrive.
4. Add TEXT and the gag-library jokes.
5. Sound: music at about 15–20% under speech, an effect on nearly every cut, then auto-captions (fix the spelling of
   `[ALGO]` and Bahn).
6. Export 1080p, 30 fps, H.264.

## Before you upload

Fill in the blanks first, then tick the rest off on your final watch-through.

### Fill in

- [ ] `[ALGO]`: the algorithm's name
- [ ] `[TEAM]` and the team members' names for the end card
- [ ] `[OUR NUMBER]`: gross margin from the test URL
- [ ] `[BASELINE]`: a number to beat (a naive strategy, or your first attempt)
- [ ] `[X]%`: on-time rate. If it's above 90%, say so: that's the line for being ranked
- [ ] The real number of strategies, if it isn't 70

### Check

- [ ] Under 3:00, and the first 5 seconds already make someone curious
- [ ] Every number on screen matches the test URL
- [ ] No real logos or copyrighted music; the Roblox clip is your own team's
- [ ] Captions on, and readable on a phone
- [ ] The teammate in the Roblox-video joke has seen it and laughs
- [ ] Someone who hasn't seen it watched it once, and you fixed the parts where they got bored
- [ ] Exported at 1080p, and the uploaded version plays with sound

All amounts in the simulation are game euros, so the "underpaid drivers" joke stays clearly a joke. Facts about the
challenge come from Bahn's [How it works](https://spread.bahnexpress.com/how) page.
